# Scouting packets (D-043)

*"These are the teams I could have used on my journey… imagine creating scouting reports with tendencies, like a Major League Baseball scouting report or an NFL call sheet."*

The journey is over (D-042), so these read like a front office's file on a club that already played. They're **reports, not simulations**: what each player's tools are, how it tends to play, and what to call against each opponent.

One packet per chapter, generated by `engine/battle/scouting_report.py` from the canon rosters and `canon/builds.json`:
[Crystal](crystal.md) · [FireRed](firered.md) · [Emerald](emerald.md) · [Platinum](platinum.md) · [SoulSilver](soulsilver.md) · [HeartGold](heartgold.md)

## How to read a player card

- **Tools on the 20–80 scale**, like a scout's: 50 is average, 60 plus, 70 plus-plus, 80 elite.
  - Power is the top attacking stat.
  - Speed is base Speed.
  - Bulk is HP × Defense and HP × Sp. Def.
  - Coverage is how many types the moveset hits super-effectively.
  - Utility counts strategic roles: setup, recovery, pivot, status, priority…
- **Era-adjusted:** every grade is measured against all fully evolved Pokémon of that game's generation. A Crystal 60 and a HeartGold 60 mean the same thing, the way OPS+ lets you compare eras.
- **Present / peak:** present is the build on record. Peak is the best of that and the strongest set it can learn (D-024), leaving out rejected moves and moves that need a recharge or a charge turn. This is the "at the peak of their powers" question.
- **OFP** is the plain average of the tools.
- The rest of the card:
  - **Out pitch:** its best move.
  - **Pitch mix:** how many moves are physical, special or status.
  - **Approach:** its tendencies.
  - **Splits:** which kind of hitter it's sturdier against.
  - **Comps:** the most similar Pokémon of its generation.
- **1v1 vs the league:** the benchmark grid against that game's Elite Four and champions, with exact damage from Smogon's calculator at the opponent's top level. Rematches are left out.

## How to read a call sheet

For every Pokémon the opponent can send:
- **the call:** the quickest clean win, with the move and its damage range;
- **the backup;**
- **don't send:** members it knocks out in one hit.

The sheet ends with the opener, the closers (priority moves), the anchors (most 1v1 wins) and any Pokémon with no answer.
