## How to read a player card

The card follows the layout of a scouting form:
- **Header:** the species, its types, its height and weight, and the OFP in a ring (present, with peak below it).
- **The form row:**
  - **Projects as:** setup sweeper, wall, pivot, speed attacker, tank, closer, utility or attacker.
  - **Risk:** low, medium or high, with the reasons: frail on one side, a low-accuracy out pitch, a 4× weakness, or playing without a signature item.
  - **The 1v1 record** against the league, and the build's source.
- **The take:** one typed line built only from the card's own numbers.
- **Tools on the 20–80 scale**, present and peak, like a scout's Pres/Fut columns.
  - Power is the top attacking stat.
  - Speed is base Speed.
  - Bulk is HP × Defense and HP × Sp. Def.
  - Coverage is how many types the moveset hits super-effectively.
  - Utility counts strategic roles: setup, recovery, pivot, status, priority…
  - Grades are **era-adjusted**: each is measured against all fully evolved Pokémon of that game's generation, so a Crystal 60 and a HeartGold 60 mean the same thing, like OPS+.
  - Peak is the best of the build on record and the strongest set it can learn (D-024), leaving out rejected moves and moves that need a recharge or a charge turn.
- **Arsenal:** every move as a pitch, with its type, its physical/special category in that era (Bite is special in Gen 3), power, accuracy and a **20–80 pitch grade** against the generation's best same-type hits. Each pitch also has a job:
  - out pitch, STAB or coverage, with the types a coverage move adds;
  - closer, setup, recovery or status.
- **Splits by type:** what its best hit does to each of the 17 types, and what each type does to it.
- **Tough matchups:** non-legendary Pokémon of its generation that hit it super-effectively and take no better than neutral from anything it carries.
- **Comps:** the most similar Pokémon of its generation by stat shape.
- **1v1 vs the league:** the benchmark grid against that game's Elite Four and champions, with exact damage from Smogon's calculator at the opponent's top level. Rematches are left out.

## How to read a call sheet

Situation boxes sit around one call table, like a laminated play sheet:
- **Opener:** they lead with X; here's the call.
- **Lead with:** our member that wins against their lead and the most of the rest.
- **Their best player:** the one that beats the most of ours.
- **If they set up:** each of their Pokémon carrying a boosting move, and the answer.
- **Closers:** our priority moves.
- **Last one standing:** our anchor.
- **No answer:** any of theirs nobody beats.

For every Pokémon they can send, the table shows:
- their moves and item;
- **the call:** the quickest clean win, with the damage it does, what it takes back, the hits-to-KO, and who moves first;
- **the backup;**
- **don't send:** members it knocks out in one hit.

## The what-if league

This is the "what if Ash used everyone at their peak" question. Every journey team meets every other at Lv 100, under the same Gen 4 rules, with 31 IVs and builds as recorded. A team's score is how many of the 36 one-on-ones it wins, and a series goes to the team that wins more of them than its opponent does in return. It's a matchup read, not a simulated battle.

## Early reads

- **HeartGold:** Snorlax grades lowest on tools (OFP 48) but has the best line in the league, 27-5. Tools aren't production. Crobat is the best athlete (Speed 70).
- **SoulSilver:** Pikachu is 5-27 without a Light Ball and 17-15 with one. The item is the whole player.
- **Platinum:** Garchomp is the ace (OFP 54/56, 24-2). Against Cynthia's Garchomp the call is Weavile's Ice Punch, and Lucario, Infernape and Magnezone all go down in one hit.
- **Typhlosion:** a mixed attacker with its nature still open (Q4). The card flags that any nature that cuts Attack or Sp. Atk weakens half its set.
- **What-if league:** Platinum wins it (4 of 5 series, .556). FireRed, Emerald and HeartGold go 3-2. Crystal wins the most one-on-ones after Platinum (94) but only 2 series. SoulSilver goes 0-5, and most of that is Pikachu without its Light Ball.
- **Roles:** HeartGold is the most balanced room on paper: a pivot (Crobat), a setup sweeper (Tyranitar), a closer (Mamoswine), an attacker (Typhlosion) and two walls (Lapras, Snorlax). Crystal's auto-filled sets (it has no builds on record) make three of its six setup sweepers, all through Curse.
